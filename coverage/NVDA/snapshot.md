## 1. What they sell and to whom

- **[F]** Self-described "data center scale AI infrastructure company." Sells GPUs plus CPUs (Grace), networking
  (NVLink, InfiniBand, Ethernet), complete rack-scale systems and software (CUDA, AI Enterprise). *10-K FY2026, Item 1*
- **[F]** Two reporting segments: **Compute & Networking** (Data Center + Automotive) and **Graphics** (GeForce gaming,
  RTX workstation). *10-K FY2026, Item 1*
- **[F]** Data Center was **$89.0B of $96.2B Q2 revenue (~92%)**, up 117% YoY. Within it, Hyperscale was $48.7B
  (+102% YoY) and ACIE (AI clouds, industrial, enterprise) grew 138%. *Q2 FY27 10-Q MD&A; CFO commentary (8-K 2026-08-26)*
- **[F]** Fabless: TSMC and Samsung make the wafers; memory comes from SK Hynix, Micron and Samsung; assembly by Hon Hai,
  Wistron and Fabrinet. Supply chain is "mainly concentrated in Asia." *10-K FY2026, Item 1, Manufacturing*
- **[F]** **Customer concentration:** one direct customer = 16% of Q2 revenue; three direct customers = 16%, 15% and 13%
  of 1H revenue. One "AI research and deployment company" contributed "a meaningful amount" of revenue indirectly
  through cloud providers. *Q2 FY27 10-Q MD&A*
- **[F]** Named competitors include AMD, Intel, Huawei, Broadcom, and the hyperscalers' in-house chips
  (Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft). *10-K FY2026, Item 1, Competition*

> **Why it matters:** NVIDIA is now essentially a single-market company (AI data centers) selling to a handful of
> very large buyers. That's why its stock trades on hyperscaler capex plans more than anything else.

The three trends that matter:
1. **[F] Growth is speeding up again.** YoY growth slowed to 56% in Jul-25, then rose for four straight quarters to
   106%. Very few companies can re-accelerate at a $300B+ revenue run rate.
2. **[F] Margins look like a software company's.** ~75% gross and ~66% operating margin. The FY26 dip to 71% gross came
   from a **$4.5B H20 inventory charge** after China export rules changed. *10-K FY2026, Item 1, Gov't Regulations*
3. **[F] Cash is lagging profits (new).** Q2 FCF was $21.4B against $59.7B of net income. Receivables reached
   **$63.1B, and DSO rose from 45 to 60 days** "due to extended payment terms on large, multi-quarter agreements
   with certain investment-grade customers." *CFO commentary Q2 FY27; 10-Q MD&A Liquidity*

> **Why it matters:** When profits grow faster than cash, analysts ask whether the company is effectively lending to
> its customers so they can buy. See section 5.

## 5. What the market is probably debating [I]

These are my inferences, written as questions to investigate rather than answers.

1. **Is NVIDIA financing its own demand?** It has $105B in lease guarantees for OpenAI, $36B in cloud buy-backs,
   extended customer payment terms (DSO 45 → 60) and a planned $500B+ financing platform. How much of revenue
   depends on NVIDIA's own balance sheet? What happens if a large AI lab can't pay?
2. **How long can this spending last?** Growth depends on a few hyperscalers and AI labs continuing to spend
   heavily. What do *their* capex guidance and AI revenues say?
3. **Will custom chips take share?** Broadcom and Marvell build chips designed in-house by the hyperscalers (TPU,
   Trainium, etc.). Does that cap NVIDIA's share or its 75% gross margin?
4. **The $279B in supply commitments:** strong confidence, or inventory risk if demand slows? (FY26's $4.5B H20 charge is
   a small example of how this can go wrong.)
5. **China:** effectively zero today. Is that already in the price, which would make any return upside?

## 6. Questions for you before we go deeper

1. **The valuation puzzle:** NVIDIA trades at 25x trailing earnings while growing 100%+. Is the market right to be
   skeptical? What would have to happen for 25x to be *expensive*?
2. **Customer financing:** after reading section 4, do the OpenAI guarantee and the extended payment terms worry
   you, or are they normal practice for a dominant supplier? (Good to compare: Cisco and Lucent's vendor financing
   in 1999–2000.)
3. **Which KPI would you track** to know whether the bull or bear case is winning? Data Center growth, gross margin,
   DSO / free cash flow conversion, or customer concentration?
